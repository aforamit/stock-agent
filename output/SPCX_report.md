## 1. Executive summary

SPCX is a newly public company (IPO June 2026 at $135.00) with a market capitalization of about $2.2T at $167.38 per share (MARKET DATA). Revenue is growing fast: Q2 2026 revenue was $7,814M, up 91.9% from $4,071M (SEC 10-Q, 2026-08-04). Free cash flow is deeply negative and worsening, and the rule engine could not produce a reliable intrinsic valuation.

- **Why AVOID / INSUFFICIENT DATA:** Average free cash flow is ≤ 0, so a DCF is "not meaningful" (Rule checks). There is no FCF forecast, no capex guidance and no margin path in the material provided, so no valuation scenarios exist (Filings, growth rationale).
- **Cash burn:** FCF was $(25,010)M in H1 2026 (computed: OCF $3,466M minus PP&E purchases $28,476M; SEC 10-Q). That compares with $(14,121)M in FY2025 and $(5,387)M in FY2024 (MARKET DATA).
- **Growth quality:** Roughly $1,600M of the Q2 revenue increase came from new AI infrastructure cloud contracts. These contracts can be terminated on 90 days' notice after the ramp period, and revenue is concentrated in a few customers (SEC 10-Q, Item 1A, Note 3).
- **Valuation:** About 96x trailing sales (computed), 17.33x book and 86.93x forward P/E (MARKET DATA). That leaves little margin for error.
- **Governance and reporting:** The dual-class structure gives public holders little recourse. The 10-Q also contains internal inconsistencies, detailed in Section 6.
- **Offsetting strengths:** A leading position in launch and LEO broadband, Connectivity income from operations of $2,844M in H1, a $47,461M backlog, and about $100,009M of cash and marketable securities (SEC 10-Q).

The qualitative work rates the moat "wide" for Space and Connectivity and management "average". A skeptic review judged the overall severity of concerns **major**. This analysis is based on the filings and data provided and has real gaps (Section 8).

## 5. Moat and management

### Moat: Wide (applies to Space and Connectivity only)

**Sources (qualitative analysis):**
- Vertical integration: Starlink launches on its own Falcon 9 rockets.
- First-mover scale in LEO broadband: 12.0M subscribers across 170 markets.
- Launch cadence: 78 launches and 1,041 tons to orbit in H1 2026.
- Spectrum and regulatory position, including the EchoStar spectrum.
- Enterprise and government revenue of $1.8B, up 108%, and a $47.5B backlog.
- Capital access, with about $100B of cash and securities.
- Connectivity operating income of $1.7B in Q2 (+79%).

**Limits on the moat:**
- The AI segment is largely resold Nvidia-based compute under cloud contracts, a commodity-like business with no clear structural advantage (qualitative).
- Starlink ARPU fell 22.4% to $66 (SEC 10-Q, MD&A). This looks more like pricing pressure than pricing power.
- Space segment softness (SEC 10-Q): H1 revenue fell 1.9%, Falcon launches fell from 45 to 37 in Q2, mass to orbit fell from 652 to 485 metric tons, and H1 segment operating income was $(1,204)M.
- The skeptic points out that the two "moat" segments earned only about $1.6B combined in H1 (computed: $2,844M − $1,204M), against $28.5B of capex. The moat does not cover the segment where most capital is going.
- Amazon Leo and others are well-funded challengers.

### Management: Average

**Strengths:** Strong execution and engineering. Operating cash flow and revenue are growing quickly.

**Concerns:**
- Capital allocation is aggressive. H1 capex was $28.5B, mostly AI data centers.
- The CFO described AI compute capital as having a sub-one-year payback. The skeptic argues this is not supported by the reported numbers: Q2 AI infrastructure revenue annualizes to about $8.8B on 1.4 GW, and the AI segment lost $(3.7)B in H1.
- Targets for 2 GW by end-2026 and about 10 GW by end-2027 appear hard to square with the stated capex pace (skeptic analysis; not confirmed by the company).
- A covenant technical default occurred in February 2026 after the xAI merger and was waived in March (SEC 10-Q, Note 9).
- Related-party failed sale-leaseback debt with Valor, whose CEO is a company director, tripled in six months.

**Governance (qualitative research):**
- Class B shares carry 10 votes each. Class B is about 88% of votes per the filings calculation, while governance sources cite about 85% for Musk. There is no sunset provision.
- The CEO, CTO and chairman can be removed only by Class B holders.
- Disputes go to binding arbitration, with jury-trial and class-action waivers.
- Texas incorporation carries a 3% ownership threshold for derivative suits. The $52.5B threshold quoted is stale at the current market cap; 3% of $2.2T is about $66B (skeptic).
- Musk's compensation package can grant up to 1B additional shares.
- Institutional investors have publicly objected.

## 6. Risks and bear case

**Company-stated risks (SEC 10-Q, Item 1A and Notes):**
- **AI infrastructure:** Revenue is concentrated in a few customers. Cloud agreements are terminable on 90 days' notice after the ramp. There is exposure to power, construction, supply-chain and permit constraints.
- **Customer concentration:** Customer A was 18.3% of Q2 revenue. Customer B was 19.5% (Note 3). The 10-Q's basis for Customer B (total revenue vs AI segment) is ambiguous. The difference is roughly $1.5B versus $0.5B.
- **Leverage and funding:** $38,433M of debt principal, including $13,329M of related-party debt. Spectrum cash payments to EchoStar will need cash or new financing.
- **Commitments:** $27,955M of non-cancelable obligations, with $22,244M in 2027. The Spectrum Transaction totals about $19.6B, including about 261.8M Class A shares at a fixed $42.40.
- **Other operating pressures:** Starlink ARPU decline, Space segment softness, and an advertising revenue decline of $59M in Q2 and $160M in H1 from the ad platform transition.
- **Litigation and regulation:** Grok-related class actions, an EC fine of EUR 120M under appeal, a $105M Vidstream verdict plus $67M interest (on appeal), a Clean Air Act suit, and privacy suits. Only $354M is accrued.
- **Deal and dilution risk:**
  - The Cursor Merger has an implied equity value of $60B, payable in stock and expected to close in Q3 2026.
  - 1,331M performance awards and 564M anti-dilutive securities are outstanding.
  - Lock-up expirations and 10b5-1 plans, including up to 225.9M shares for Valor entities, add supply.

**Skeptic bear case (severity: major):**
- **Funding gap:** The $100B cash balance is not a safety net. It faces about $18B per quarter of capex, $22.2B of 2027 obligations and the spectrum cash leg. The skeptic estimates a funding need within about 6–8 quarters, which implies new debt or equity.
- **AI plan versus capex:** H1 AI capex of $23.6B bought roughly 1 GW of year-over-year growth. Eight more GW in 2027 at similar cost implies well over $100B of 2027 AI capex (skeptic estimate).
- **Growth quality:**
  - Q1 2026 revenue growth was only about 15% (computed from H1 less Q2).
  - Excluding the roughly $1.6B AI step-up, Q2 growth is about 52% (skeptic computation).
- **Possible circularity:** Cursor will be bought with SPCX stock while a concurrent compute agreement and option exist, valued at zero. The 10-Q says the net Q2 amount was "not material".
- **Goodwill:** $11,130M of the $11,645M total sits in the loss-making AI segment, with no impairment recorded.

**Accounting and disclosure red flags (SEC 10-Q; MARKET DATA):**
- Related-party failed sale-leasebacks are recorded as debt, so cash capex understates total asset additions.
- The MD&A says no material debt principal is due until July 15, 2031. Note 9 shows $944M for the remainder of 2026, $2,402M in 2027, $2,867M in 2028, $3,422M in 2029 and $3,597M in 2030.
- The SpaceX Notes redemption spread is "20 to 30 basis points" in Note 9 and "between 0.002 and 0.003 basis points" in the MD&A.
- Debt-extinguishment loss was $1,545M in H1 on legacy debt at 9.50% and 12.50%.
- The allocation of AI compute costs between cost of revenue and R&D is judgmental and undisclosed.
- Adjusted EBITDA excludes large recurring D&A and SBC.
- The MARKET DATA `shares` field (7.696B) is Class A only, while market cap uses about 13.18B total shares. Per-share metrics based on the former would be overstated by about 71% (computed).
- Smaller tie-out differences exist in SBC and prepayment-penalty figures.

## 8. Data gaps and caveats

- No FCF forecast, capex guidance or margin targets. The growth rate is therefore null (Filings).
- The Rule 424(b)(4) prospectus and FY2025 audited annual statements were not provided.
- It is unclear whether the 2023–2025 MARKET DATA history is recast for the common-control xAI and X mergers. FY2024 net income of $791M cannot be reconciled with the provided material, and FY2025 OCF of $6,785M vs H1 2025 OCF of $351M is unexplained.
- The basis for the MARKET DATA EV/EBITDA of 700.4 (implied EBITDA of about $3.1B, computed) and for total debt of $39,708M is unknown. The debt principal also appears as both $38,433M and $39,512M in the filings summary.
- The identities of Customers A and B and of the Class B holders are not disclosed. The Customer B percentage basis is ambiguous.
- Capex beyond 2026 and the cost-allocation methodology for AI compute are not disclosed, so segment gross margin cannot be assessed.
- Yahoo Finance data has quirks. "Year Ago Sales" is blank or odd, and its intraday price is $167.20 vs MARKET DATA's $167.38.
- Q3 2026 results, the Cursor Merger terms and the final Spectrum accounting are not yet available.
- Some qualitative inputs come from secondary web sources, such as a satellite-count table and governance commentary, and are less reliable than the 10-Q.
- Skeptic figures such as 2026 capex near $65B, the 10 GW cost estimate and a 6–8 quarter funding horizon are analytical estimates and not company disclosures.
- SPCX has a very short public history, so price behavior, lock-up effects and index flows are uncertain.
