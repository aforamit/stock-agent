- **Classification:** Industrials / Rental & Leasing Services, reporting in USD (MARKET DATA). The filings extract provided no further operating description.
- **Scale (company-reported, via qualitative research):** AerCap describes itself as the industry leader in aviation leasing, with about 3,500 aircraft, engines and helicopters owned, on order or managed at 12/31/2025 ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1378789/000162828026007513/aer-20251231.htm)). - **Competitors:** SMBC Aviation Capital, Air Lease Corporation, Avolon, BOC Aviation, Dubai Aerospace Enterprise and Aviation Capital Group. The 20-F also names manufacturers, banks and airlines.
- **Industry backdrop:** Management describes demand above supply, with constrained aircraft and engine availability. Lease extension rates were 85–87% in 2026. Risks include higher oil prices, geopolitical shocks and Middle East and Asia Pacific weakness. A recovery in OEM production could ease the shortage that currently supports lease rates and asset values. No quantified industry growth rate was found.

## 5. Moat & management

**Moat: narrow** (Qualitative analysis)
- **Sources:**
  - Scale and OEM purchasing power, including delivery slots such as 15 Boeing 787s for 2030–2033.
  - Investment-grade funding, with an average cost of debt of 4.2%.
  - Remarketing capability, with 705 transactions in 2025.
  - A track record integrating ILFC and GECAS.
- **Evidence:** Q2 2026 lease yield was up 30bp and net spread up 50bp year over year, with asset sales at about a 20% gain margin ([Q2 2026 call](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/AER/pressreleases/3623571/aercap-aer-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).
- **Caveat:** Part of this reflects an industry-wide aircraft shortage, not only AerCap-specific strengths. The claim of buying narrowbody slots 16–17% below competitors is management's and unverified. Aircraft are cyclical, capital-intensive assets, and FY2022 net income was -$726M after the Russia charge.

**Management: strong** (Qualitative analysis; the Skeptic challenges how well this is tested)
- Since 2023, AerCap repurchased over 93M shares (about 40% of those outstanding) for roughly $8B. It raised the quarterly dividend from $0.27 to $0.40 and approved a new $1B buyback through June 2027 ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/aercap-approves-1-billion-share-repurchase-program-through-june-2027-ce785bd2dc81ff26)). The summary cites a September 16, 2026 approval date, while a cited MarketBeat URL is dated 2026-04-29. This discrepancy is not resolved here.
- Management reports leverage of 2.05x against a 2.5x target and about $22B of liquidity. The 2026 adjusted EPS guide is about $16.80, of which about $2.80 comes from asset-sale gains. Adjusted ROE is 18%.
- CEO Aengus Kelly has led the company since 2011. About $2.5B of pretax Russia insurance recoveries are expected against the $2.7B pretax net charge taken in 2022.
- **Governance flags:** key-person risk with no disclosed succession plan, ongoing Russia litigation with appeal risk, the contingent VASP case in Brazil, and no CEO pay data found. No specific governance scandal was identified.

## 6. Risks & bear case

Drawn from the Skeptic review, whose panel severity was **minor** on all three panel assessments:

1. **Earnings quality:** Net income was 1.39x operating income in FY2025 and 1.51x in FY2023. Likely contributors are Russia recoveries, gains on sale and tax items, but the filings provided no breakdown.
2. **Gains dependence:** About 17% of guided 2026 adjusted EPS comes from aircraft-sale gains in a supply-constrained market. Management declined to guide to second-half gains.
3. **Cash conversion:** Revenue rose about 21% from 2022 to 2025, while operating cash flow rose about 4%. Cumulative FCF was about -$2.83B against about +$8.99B of net income.
4. **Buybacks funded by debt and asset sales:** FCF has been negative every year since 2023, while about $8B went to repurchases plus more in 2026. FCF as defined excludes asset-sale proceeds, so it overstates the cash burn.
5. **Leverage and refinancing:** Debt is about 1.9x market cap. Debt/equity is 2.33 on the data provided, versus management's 2.05x on an unreconciled company definition. Net spread is the profit engine, so refinancing at higher rates or wider spreads would squeeze it.
6. **Cycle and residual values:** The current shortage could ease if OEM output recovers. Airline credit stress, oil prices or geopolitics could compress lease rates and gain margins together.
7. **Tail risks:** Russia recoveries still face appeal, and the VASP case and key-person risk remain.

**Balancing view (Skeptic):** These are structural, cyclical features of a large investment-grade lessor, not signs of fraud. The Skeptic's own conclusion was that this is "a valuation and cycle-risk issue, not a reason to avoid the stock regardless of price." The fixed AVOID / INSUFFICIENT DATA label reflects that valuation could not be established reliably, not a finding of impairment.
